## 1. Design decision: one Form per rater

Your presentation schedule (seed `20260914`) is a **fixed, pre-committed** order per rater, not something to randomize live. Google Forms' built-in shuffle would generate a *new*, unregistered order — that breaks the pre-registration. So: build one Form, then duplicate it 1–2 times (Forms → ⋮ → "Make a copy") and swap the Card 1 / Card 2 content order per the table in §3.

You need **minimum 2 raters** (you as `rater_1`, non-blind, disclosed as the author, plus one external person as `rater_2`) and **target 3** if you can get a second external person (`rater_3`). Don't decide the count based on early results — collect what you can recruit and report the realized number.

## 2. Form settings

- **Title:** "Dataset Discovery Tool Evaluation" (neutral — don't reveal this is about your ranking system)
- **Settings → Responses:** Turn **OFF** "Collect email addresses" and **OFF** "Limit to 1 response" (no sign-in required) — this keeps the form anonymous, consistent with the frozen schema collecting no PII.
- **Settings → Presentation:** Turn OFF "Shuffle question order."
- Do **not** send yourself a copy of raters' responses labeled with their real name anywhere — assign `rater_2` / `rater_3` as pseudonyms only, in your own notes, never on the form.

**Optional consent blurb** (first thing on the form):
> This is a short evaluation for an academic capstone project. You'll review 7 pairs of dataset descriptions and rate each on a few short scales — about 15–20 minutes. Your responses are anonymous and used only in aggregate. Participation is voluntary; you may stop at any time.

## 4. Intro section content (verbatim from your frozen instrument)

**Purpose of the Study**
> In this study, you will evaluate different representations of dataset information for machine learning tasks. When selecting a dataset for a real-world task, practitioners must assess whether a candidate dataset fits their technical requirements, data modality, quality expectations, and licensing constraints.

**Your Role as an Evaluator**
> You will be presented with 7 comparison sets spanning three machine learning tasks (Text Classification, Tabular Classification, and Image Classification). For each comparison: (1) Read the Task Description and requirements carefully. (2) Examine Card 1 and provide your ratings independently across three dimensions. (3) Examine Card 2 (an alternative representation of the same dataset) and provide your ratings independently across the same three dimensions.

**Neutrality & Blinding Guidelines**
> Card representations are generated by alternative automated tooling — you will not be told which pipeline generated which card. Rate each card on its own merits; do not assume one format is intended to be "better" or "worse." Presentation order is randomized and varies across comparisons.

## 8. After collection: transcribing into your audited capture tool

Don't paste the raw Google Sheet export directly into `m8_human_eval_responses.csv` — route it through `scripts/capture_m8_human_eval_response.py`, which validates every field against the frozen manifest and preserves the append-only guarantee your Sonnet audit already verified.

For each of the 14 rows per rater (7 comparisons × 2 cards), run:

```bash
python3 scripts/capture_m8_human_eval_response.py \
  --rater-id rater_2 \
  --task-name <task_name> \
  --comparison-id <comparison_id> \
  --dataset-id <dataset_id> \
  --format-presented <format_a_plain|format_b_evidence> \
  --presentation-order <first|second> \
  --trust-score <1-5> \
  --understanding-score <1-5> \
  --actionability-score <1-5> \
  --notes "<their optional note, or empty string>"
```

`--format-presented` / `--presentation-order`: whichever card was **Card 1** in that rater's row from the §3 table gets `--presentation-order first`; whichever was **Card 2** gets `second`. The format flag is `format_a_plain` for the plain metadata card, `format_b_evidence` for the recommendation card — regardless of which position it landed in.

The tool will reject the row outright if any value doesn't match the frozen schedule for that rater/comparison, so a transcription typo gets caught immediately rather than silently entering the record.
